# 04 — Pricing and launch timing

## Pricing

| Tier | Price | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Migration | Free, one time | Supplier reconstruction. The hook. Never charge for this. |
| Starter | $29/mo | POs, receiving, reorder suggestions, 1 location |
| Standard | $49/mo | Adds the supplier follow-up agent, multi-location, unlimited suppliers |

Anchor against what they lost and what they are being offered: Stocky was free,
and the suites are $100–300/mo. At $29–49 you are the only option that does not
feel like a category change. That is the position — do not drift upmarket to chase
the first enterprise-shaped merchant who asks.

**Do not price at $9.** At $9 you need hundreds of merchants to feel anything,
and cheap customers file the most support tickets. $29 is the floor for B2B.
A hundred merchants at $49 is $4,900/mo with no team, and that is the realistic
twelve-month target.

**Charge from install, with a 14-day trial.** Free-forever tiers attract people
who will never pay and bury you in support. The free thing is the migration,
which is one-time work that creates switching cost — that is a hook, not a tier.

## The two waves

This is the non-obvious part of the timing, and getting it wrong wastes your Q4.

**Wave 1 — now through late October.** Merchants cut off on 31 August who are
still limping along on spreadsheets. Acute, urgent, small. These are your design
partners, not your volume.

**The freeze — November through December.** Retailers do not change inventory
systems during their highest-revenue weeks. BFCM lands 27–30 November 2026. A
launch on 15 November converts nothing, and you will misread a calendar problem
as a product problem and rewrite something that was fine. Plan for this.

**Wave 2 — January through March 2027.** The extension cohort, whose deadline is
March 2027, plus everyone who survived Q4 on a spreadsheet and resolved to fix it
in the new year. This is your real launch window, and it is also when retailers
do their annual tooling review.

So: **build and get design partners now; launch properly in January.** Use
November and December for support, listing quality, and the content that will
rank by January — not for a push that the calendar will eat.

## Distribution, in priority order

**1. The community threads.** Highest-conversion channel you have, and it costs
nothing. A founder replying personally in a thread about a problem they have
solved beats a content team every time. Keep replying after you launch.

**2. App store search.** Optimise the listing for what a stranded merchant
actually types: "Stocky replacement", "purchase orders", "reorder". Your first
reviews matter enormously — ask every design partner, individually, after you have
visibly solved something for them.

**3. One piece of content that is not a competitor's.** Everyone is writing "Best
Stocky Alternatives". Do not. Write the thing only you can write: the honest
field guide to what actually broke on 1 September, what native Shopify POs can and
cannot do, and when the answer is "you don't need my app." That is linkable,
quotable in threads, and it establishes that you are the person who knows this
subject rather than a vendor with a funnel.

**4. Do not buy ads.** You cannot outbid inventory suites with venture funding
for these keywords, and you should not try.

## Metrics that matter

Track four things and ignore the rest:

- **Install → first PO sent.** Your real activation metric. If it is under 40%,
  onboarding is broken, not the product.
- **Migration → paid conversion.** Tells you whether the wedge pulls people into
  the recurring product, which is the whole thesis.
- **Month-2 retention.** Month 1 is migration relief; month 2 is whether the
  product is useful. This is the number that decides if there is a business here.
- **Support minutes per merchant per week.** You are one person with school. If
  this exceeds about 15 minutes, you cannot grow past thirty merchants, and the
  fix is product, not effort.
